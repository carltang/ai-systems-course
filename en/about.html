AboutHow this course came about

What it is. Lecture notes for people with an engineering background who want a systematic understanding of AI training systems: principles first, connections between them, and simple examples and analogies wherever possible. The hands-on exercises are all optional.

How it was written. The explanations and all the drawings were written and revised round by round with the help of AI (Anthropic's Claude), to standards set by one learner. The content draws on published books, papers and official documentation, retold in our own words; the "Sources" under each principle list what it rests on. Books and papers are given only by name and chapter: please read a copy you have obtained legitimately.

Where to keep your wits about you.

  • The source walkthroughs (PyTorch, NCCL) were written from papers and documentation, without being checked line by line against a specific version. When reading source code, go by the version you have.
  • The order-of-magnitude figures throughout (latency, bandwidth, speed-ups) are orders of magnitude, not measurements from a particular machine.
  • The four troubleshooting cases of part 11 are composite cases written to tie the methods together; their numbers are consistent, but they are not records of real measurements.
  • The YAML in part 8 is illustrative, and the fields depend on your cluster's version; the parts on combining large models with reinforcement learning reflect practice around 2025, which is changing fast.

If you find a mistake, it is quite likely a real one.

Authors. Zongyao Tang and Claude. The explanations retell published books, papers and official documentation in our own words, and the drawings and interactive demos were made afresh. The division of work: Zongyao Tang set the course's goals, standards and trade-offs; Claude wrote the explanations, drawings and demos to those requirements, which he then reviewed round by round, asking for changes, before they were finalised.

The English edition. Translated by AI (Anthropic's Claude) from the Chinese edition, passage by passage; where the two differ, the Chinese is the reference. Its content, drawings and interactive demos match the Chinese one for one. The EN / 中文 button at the top of every page switches language, stays at the same place, and remembers your choice.
