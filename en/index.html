AI Systems: From Deep Learning Principles to GPUs and Distributed Training

Zongyao Tang & Claude

Translated by AI (Anthropic's Claude) from the Chinese edition. The Chinese original is the reference.

A course that explains the principles: what one training step actually goes through inside the machine, where it is slow when it is slow, and how to trace a symptom layer by layer to its cause. It starts from neural networks and PyTorch, and goes through GPUs and CUDA, operators and kernels, high-speed interconnects and NCCL, to distributed training; the three parts on clusters, inference and reinforcement learning are optional.

If this is your first time, start with part 0 (3 units): it is the map for everything that follows. Each unit's explanation is made of a few principles, each with an analogy, a "so", the principle it builds on and where it is used later.

Part 0 · The big picture and measurement

  • 0.1The journey of one training stepA map for the whole course: which stages one training step passes through, the layers the system is made of, and the few kinds of "slow".
  • 0.2The language of measurement: utilisation, throughput and MFUTell apart what utilisation, throughput and MFU each measure; know how to set up a baseline and how to split the time across the stages of the pipeline.
  • 0.3A refresher on systems fundamentalsPick up the operating-system and computer-organisation knowledge used later: the memory hierarchy, pinned memory, DMA, the kernel network stack, processes and threads.

Part 1 · Principles of deep learning

  • 1.1Neural networks and backpropagationWrite a neural network that trains, from scratch, and really understand how gradients are computed.
  • 1.2Loss, optimisation and generalisationWhy a model can learn at all, what decides how fast it learns, and why it can overfit.
  • 1.3Training in practice, and diagnosisGet a model actually trained: how to schedule the learning rate, what regularisation guards against, how to see problems in the loss curves, and in what order to look when something is wrong.
  • 1.4The TransformerBuild by hand a small GPT that generates text, and understand attention, residual connections and layer normalisation.
  • 1.5Reproducing GPT-2: from model to efficient trainingTurn the small GPT into a real training job, and meet mixed precision, FlashAttention, torch.compile and multi-GPU training for the first time.

Part 2 · The PyTorch execution stack

  • 2.1Tensors and memoryWhat a tensor is in memory, which operations copy no data, how PyTorch manages GPU memory, and where the memory goes during training.
  • 2.2The autograd engineHow the computation graph is recorded during the forward pass, and how backward() walks along it to compute the gradients.
  • 2.3From a line of Python to a kernelFollow one operator call from Python to the GPU, and see what each layer in between does and how long it takes.
  • 2.4The data supplyHow a batch is prepared and delivered to the GPU, and which stretch of the pipeline each DataLoader argument tunes.
  • 2.5The three-layer stack of torch.compileHow torch.compile turns a piece of Python into a few fused kernels, and when it stops working.
  • 2.6Mixed precision and speeding up single-GPU trainingGather up the remaining speed-ups on a single GPU: mixed precision, fused optimizers, specialised libraries, simplifying the model.

Part 3 · The GPU and the CUDA execution model

  • 3.1The GPU environment and a first performance analysisSet up a CUDA development environment, and learn to use the profiler to see where a PyTorch program is really slow.
  • 3.2The first CUDA kernelUnderstand threads, thread blocks and grids, and be able to write and launch a simple CUDA kernel correctly on your own.
  • 3.3Compute and memory architectureKnow what SMs, warps and shared memory are, and use tiling to write a matrix multiplication that is clearly faster.
  • 3.4The CUDA performance checklistMaster a checklist of kernel optimisations that works if you follow it: coalesced memory access, occupancy, branch divergence, thread coarsening.
  • 3.5Parallel patterns: reduction and scanWrite an efficient parallel reduction, and understand how "seemingly serial" computations like scan are parallelised.
  • 3.6Streams, asynchrony and CUDA GraphsHow the CPU hands tasks to the GPU: asynchronous queues, streams, synchronisation points, and how to reduce the overhead of giving orders.

Part 4 · Operators and kernels

  • 4.1Taking a Transformer apart into operatorsWrite the Transformer of 1.4 in plain CUDA: from naive kernels to training and inference that run end to end.
  • 4.2Operator fusionWhy operator fusion removes unnecessary memory reads and writes, and how to read the fused kernels a compiler generates.
  • 4.3Getting started with TritonWrite kernels in Triton that come close to hand-written CUDA in performance, and get a feel for "programming by blocks".
  • 4.4Flash AttentionUnderstand and implement FlashAttention: tiling + online softmax, cutting the memory reads and writes of attention.
  • 4.5The limits of matrix multiplication, and Tensor CoresOptimise the kernel of 4.1 step by step, and learn to do matrix multiplication with Tensor Cores.

Part 5 · High-speed interconnects

  • 5.1Interconnects inside a machine: PCIe, NVLink and NVSwitchHow GPUs, CPUs and network cards are connected inside a multi-GPU server, how wide each road is, and how to check.
  • 5.2RDMA and InfiniBandHow RDMA bypasses the kernel to read and write the other side's memory directly, what InfiniBand and RoCE each are, and which counters to look at when a link has a problem.
  • 5.3Cluster networks: topology, traffic and congestionWhat the network between hundreds or thousands of GPUs looks like, what is special about training traffic, and how congestion arises and is controlled.

Part 6 · Collective communication and NCCL

  • 6.1Collective communication and NCCLUnderstand the algorithms and bandwidth model of collective operations such as all-reduce, and be able to use NCCL and torch.distributed.
  • 6.2Inside NCCL: topology, channels and protocolsWork out what one all-reduce goes through inside NCCL, as groundwork for reading NCCL's logs and source.
  • 6.3Measuring and troubleshooting communicationLearn to judge whether communication is fast: how to measure it, how to read NCCL's logs, and in what order to check when something is wrong.

Part 7 · Distributed training

  • 7.1Data parallelism with DDPUse DDP to scale training to many GPUs, and understand gradient synchronisation and the overlap of computation with communication.
  • 7.2ZeRO and FSDPUnderstand the idea of sharding optimizer state, gradients and parameters, and be able to use FSDP to train a model that does not fit on one GPU.
  • 7.3Model parallelism and TorchTitanLearn how tensor, pipeline and context parallelism are combined, and be able to read a modern framework for training large models.
  • 7.4Checkpoints, fault tolerance and large-scale data loadingUnderstand how long, large-scale training copes with machine failures, what a checkpoint must contain and how often to save one, and how to read huge amounts of data efficiently.

Part 8 · Clusters and platforms (optional)

  • 8.1The core objects of KubernetesWork out how a training job handed to Kubernetes becomes a set of processes on several machines, and what to look at when it will not start.
  • 8.2GPUs in the cluster: devices, scheduling and queueingWork out how GPUs become resources that can be requested in Kubernetes, what decides where a job's GPUs are placed, and how jobs queue when many people share a cluster.

Part 9 · Inference systems (optional)

  • 9.1LLM inference and servingUnderstand the KV cache, batch scheduling and the main optimisations of inference engines.
  • 9.2Quantisation and low precisionUnderstand the principles and costs of int8, int4 and fp8 quantisation, and be able to write a simple quantisation kernel.
  • 9.3Inference engines: batching, memory and schedulingWork out what an inference engine does beyond the model, and why the same model on a different engine can differ several times in throughput.

Part 10 · Reinforcement learning (optional)

  • 10.1What reinforcement learning isBuild the basic picture of reinforcement learning from scratch: what problem it solves, and how it fundamentally differs from supervised learning.
  • 10.2Value and policy: two routesWork out the thinking behind each of the two main families of reinforcement learning methods, and why PPO became the most commonly used algorithm.
  • 10.3The shape of reinforcement learning systemsWork out what a reinforcement learning training system looks like, and how its bottlenecks differ from those of large-model training.
  • 10.4How industry uses reinforcement learningLearn the main uses of reinforcement learning in industry today, especially its relationship with large models, and what problems suit it.

Part 11 · Capstone project

  • 11.1Capstone projectWalk through the whole course with four troubleshooting cases: measure first, then break it down, find the biggest part, change only one thing. The hands-on project is optional.
